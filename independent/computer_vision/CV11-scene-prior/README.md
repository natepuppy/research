# CV11: The presence head is a scene classifier — SAM 3 says yes to what the scene predicts and no to what it does not

**Question.** SAM 3 decides whether a concept is in the image with one global presence token, and the paper's reason for the design is that recognition needs context from the whole image. Context cuts both ways. A scene that usually contains a concept is evidence for it whether or not it is there, and a scene that never contains one is evidence against it even when it is. The claim here is that SAM 3's presence head has learned the scene prior and the proposal queries have not. Three predictions follow, all on natural images with natural co-occurrence statistics, no edits. First, among concepts that annotators verified absent, the presence logit rises with how strongly the categories that are present predict the absent one, and this survives controlling for how similar the absent phrase is to the present ones in text-embedding space, so it is the scene and not a near-synonym doing the work. Second, among concepts that are present, the chance that presence comes out negative (the object is dropped) is higher when the scene does not predict the concept, at a fixed object size. Third, the dissociation: the proposal queries' own match scores do not move with the scene prior the way the presence logit does, in either population. If the three hold, SAM 3 carries the "elephant in the room" failure of closed-set detectors into open-vocabulary segmentation, and it carries it in one named component, which is where any fix has to go. A second model without a presence token, YOLO-World with the same 1,203-class vocabulary, is run on the same images and the same statistic is reported for its maximum class score, to say whether the explicit global token concentrates the prior or merely inherits it.

Prior work: SAM 3 (presence token, "contextual cues from the entire image are important" for recognition). Context bias in closed-set detectors: Rosenfeld et al. 2018 (the elephant in the room), Singh et al. 2020 (don't judge an object by its context). ORIC (2025) builds incongruous object-context pairs with an LLM and CLIP and evaluates 18 VLMs and two open-vocabulary detectors on yes/no recognition; it does not use natural co-occurrence statistics, does not separate a recognition head from a localization head, and does not include SAM 3. CAFE (2026) edits the surroundings of an object to plant a misleading context and finds SAM 3's presence head fooled on ontological but not contextual edits; its contexts are synthetic and its prompts are counterfactual pairs. CODet (ICCV 2025) injects co-occurrence as a prior to help open-vocabulary detectors, the opposite direction of this paper. CV8 in this repository measured text-only presence on verified-absent LVIS categories (1.5 % false alarms) and present ones (8 % misses); this paper asks what predicts which ones.

**Status.** Measured on 500 LVIS val images on the 3080 (SAM 3 fp16 and YOLO-World v2-S, 5,584 pairs of which 1,845 verified-absent and 1,542 present pass the N(c) ≥ 20 filter, 10 minutes). **INCONCLUSIVE under the pre-registered test: C1 passes 8/8, C2 fails 1/8, C3 passes 6/8.** C1: on verified-absent pairs the presence logit tracks the scene prior, Spearman 0.320, and 0.265 after partialling out CLIP text similarity to the present categories (similarity alone: 0.228, and 0.135 after partialling out context); the context score predicts a false alarm with AUROC 0.745. By context quartile (post-hoc table, `absent_by_context_quartile_posthoc.csv`), the false-alarm rate is 0.2 %, 0.7 %, 1.3 %, 3.3 % from the least to the most scene-predicted absent categories, a 15× range, and mean presence rises from −6.5 to −4.5. The false alarms read as scene inferences: saltshaker and tongs in kitchens, calculator and telephone at desks, weathervane, wall socket, fire extinguisher. C2: misses do not follow the scene. Within size bins, presence < 0 for 8.2 % of present pairs in the bottom context tercile and 6.5 % in the top (ratio 1.26, bound 1.5); by quartile 7.8 / 7.8 / 8.3 / 6.7 %. The prior adds yeses; it does not take them away. C3: on absent pairs the strongest proposal's own score correlates with context at 0.161, half the presence head's 0.320; on present pairs neither correlates (presence −0.001, matched query −0.097), so the present-pair half of C3 passes vacuously and should be read with C2. YOLO-World with the same vocabulary: on absent pairs its maximum class confidence correlates with context at 0.166 (partial 0.167), the same as SAM 3's local queries and half of SAM 3's presence head; its miss rate (max confidence < 0.25) is 60.2 % bottom tercile vs 51.2 % top. By frequency, the absent-pair correlation is 0.31 frequent, 0.19 common, 0.14 rare. Not run: SA-Co, a per-category or per-scene recalibration of presence, cropping the proposal to remove context, any model other than these two.

What the result is. SAM 3's explicit recognition token carries the scene prior at twice the strength of its own localization queries and of a detector without such a token, and the prior shows up only as false alarms on scene-typical absent concepts, not as misses on atypical present ones. The asymmetry is consistent with the training signal. SAM 3's hard negatives are, by the paper's definition (§ A, "Hard Negatives"), phrases not in the image for which a previous generation of SAM 3 predicted masks, and 88.5 % of SA-Co/HQ phrases are negatives; that teaches the presence head to reject lookalikes, and a scene-typical absent concept with nothing in the image resembling it is seldom mined as one. The untested prediction is that adding scene-sampled negatives (phrases drawn from the co-occurrence prior of the present categories) would remove the effect.

## Pre-registered kill test (written before any measurement)

Design. LVIS v1 val annotations on the COCO val2017 images on disk: the first 500 images by id with ≥ 1 verified-absent category, ≥ 1 polygon instance of area ≥ 32², ≥ 1 exhaustively annotated present category. SAM 3 fp16, threshold 0.5, phrase = first synonym with underscores replaced by spaces. Per image: **present** pairs are the exhaustively annotated present categories by total area, up to 10; **absent** pairs are up to 10 verified-absent categories drawn with a fixed per-image seed. Per pair, from one text-only decoder pass on the cached full-image features: the presence logit; the maximum over queries of σ(logit) (the strongest local evidence); and for present pairs the **matched query score**, the maximum σ(logit) over proposals with mask IoU > 0.5 to the largest instance, 0 if none, with the largest instance's COCO size bin (small < 32², medium, large > 96²).

Scene prior. Co-occurrence is estimated from all 19,809 LVIS val images using every annotated category (positive labels are reliable in a federated dataset; absence is not needed here), leaving the test image out. For a pair (image i, category c) with present set P_i (all annotated categories of i except c): PMI(c, p) = log [ P(c | p) / P(c) ] with P(c | p) = (N(c,p) + 5·P(c)) / (N(p) + 5). The **context score** is max over p ∈ P_i of PMI(c, p). The **text similarity** control is the maximum cosine between CLIP ViT-B/32 text embeddings of "a photo of a {phrase}" for c and for each p ∈ P_i. Scored tests use pairs whose category has N(c) ≥ 20 in LVIS val; all pairs are reported.

Second model. YOLO-World v2-S with the 1,203 phrases as its vocabulary, confidence floor 0.001, up to 1,000 detections. Its presence analog is the maximum confidence over boxes of class c (0 if none); its matched analog is the maximum confidence over boxes with box IoU > 0.5 to the largest instance.

Statistics. Spearman rank correlation ρ. Partial Spearman: rank-transform the three variables, regress presence and context on similarity, correlate the residuals. 8 bootstrap resamples of images; a criterion passes if it holds in ≥ 6 of 8.

- **C1 (false alarms follow the scene).** On absent pairs: ρ(context, presence logit) ≥ 0.20, and the partial ρ controlling for text similarity ≥ 0.10.
- **C2 (misses follow the scene, at fixed size).** On present pairs, within each size bin of the largest instance, split by context terciles computed over all present pairs. The pooled veto rate (presence < 0) in the bottom tercile, weighting bins by their pair count, is ≥ 1.5× the pooled veto rate in the top tercile, and the bottom-tercile pooled rate is ≥ 0.05.
- **C3 (the prior is in the presence head, not the queries).** On absent pairs, ρ(context, max query score) ≤ 0.5 × ρ(context, presence logit). On present pairs, ρ(context, matched query score) ≤ 0.5 × ρ(context, presence logit). Both.

SUPPORTED if C1, C2 and C3. INCONCLUSIVE if exactly two. KILLED otherwise. Reported, not scored: AUROC of the context score for predicting presence ≥ 0 on absent pairs; the same statistics with mean PMI in place of max; by frequency bin (r/c/f); and for YOLO-World, ρ(context, logit of max class confidence) on absent pairs, the C2 ratio with "miss" defined as max class confidence < 0.25, and the matched-vs-max dissociation.

What would make this a null: presence tracking text similarity rather than context (the partial correlation vanishes: the false alarms are near-synonyms, not scene inferences); veto rates not differing by context within size bins (the misses in CV10 are about resolution alone); or the query scores moving with context as much as presence does (the decoupling did not put the prior in one place).
