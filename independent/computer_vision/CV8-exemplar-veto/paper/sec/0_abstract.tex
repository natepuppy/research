\begin{abstract}
\sam{} decides whether a prompted concept is in the image with a presence head and multiplies every proposal's score by its output; this is what lets the model return nothing for an absent concept.
We show that a positive exemplar box removes this ability.
On 500 held-out LVIS images, for categories that the annotations verify to be absent, the presence logit is non-negative in 1.6\% of text-only prompts and in 99.5\% once one positive box is added, and the boxed object is returned as the absent concept in 96.8\% of cases.
The effect does not depend on what the box contains: a box on background gives the same result in 99.6\% of cases, as do shrunken, enlarged and shifted boxes, while a lone negative box pins the head at its lower bound.
This is consistent with the published training protocol, in which positive exemplars are drawn from instances of concepts that are present.
A training-free fix, gating the exemplar pass on the text-only presence at the cost of one extra decoder pass over cached image features, cuts false-positive episodes from 99.5\% to 6.4\% at a cost of 0.002 mIoU, and cuts false counts on FSC-147 from 85.5\% to 4.3\%.
Used to verify detector boxes, the gated score separates correct from class-absent detections better than the detectors' own confidence (AUROC 0.96 against 0.90--0.93) for two detectors.
All criteria were fixed before each run; 23 of 27 passed, and we report the four that failed.
\end{abstract}
