\paragraph{Failed criteria.}
Four of 27 criteria failed (\cref{tab:criteria}): the AUROC form of the main claim (K1, \cref{sec:failure}), the multiplicative gate on LVIS (L3) and on detector boxes (D2), and the geometry gate (H4).
Each failure changed what we claim.
The claim about presence is made at the decision level, the gate we recommend is the hard one, and we do not claim that any gate cleans up detector errors.

\paragraph{One model, one implementation.}
We tested one checkpoint of one model, through the Transformers port and not the reference code.
The scoring rule we use is the port's own.
A port-specific defect is unlikely to produce the label-dependent pattern of \cref{tab:controls}, but we have not excluded it by running the reference implementation.
We did not evaluate on SA-Co (the release we had access to contains annotations without the images), on the later \sam{}.1 checkpoint, or on video.

\paragraph{Precision.}
All main runs are in half precision.
We repeated the absent-phrase conditions in single precision on the first 100 held-out images, with criteria fixed in advance.
The rates with $\spres \geq 0$ are identical (1.7\% text only, 98.3\% with the box), the gate decision agrees in all 300 absent-phrase episodes, and the logits differ by 0.009 on average.

\paragraph{Scope of the prompts.}
Exemplars are ground-truth boxes of the largest object, detector boxes, or FSC-147 exemplars; we did not test cross-image exemplars, although they reach the model as the same kind of token.
Absent phrases are random draws from the verified-absent list, so most are unrelated to the scene.
Confusable phrases would raise the text-only false-positive rate and with it the gate's residual, as the detector experiment shows.
The mIoU cost of the gate is measured where the phrase names the largest object; for small or rare targets it may be higher.

\paragraph{Cause.}
The training-protocol account of \cref{sec:mechanism} is an inference from the published description and from behaviour.
We did not retrain or probe the model's internals.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\sam{}'s presence head is its means of saying that a concept is not there, and a positive exemplar turns it off.
The effect is close to total, does not depend on what is in the box, reverses with the exemplar's label, and appears in every use of exemplars we tried.
A text-only presence pass over the same image features restores most of the rejection for the cost of one decoder pass, and makes \sam{} a useful verifier of whether a detector's class is in the image at all.
The remedy at the source is likely to be in training: pair positive exemplars with absent concepts, and evaluate exemplar prompts on images where the answer is that nothing matches.
