4. Reading. At fixed data and update count, within-batch correlation costs 0-8 AUC points (win1 vs iid; 4/16 win1 wins at
   K=100, p=0.08; 4/16 at K=2500). The textbook decorrelation effect is small. The large effect is the opposite of the
   textbook's: with a fast target (K=100) training on the most recent 32 transitions (no replay) beats uniform replay by 40 AUC
   points and 70 final-return points (15/16 and 14/16 wins), while with a slow target (K=2500) it is the worst by AUC (-32)
   and equal at the end. The learning curves show why: at K=100 every replay sampler peaks at 5k steps (~165) and then
   declines to 80-120 (the reach-ceiling-then-collapse pattern of R11), while the recent-data learner keeps climbing to 180.
   At K=2500 nothing moves before 10k (the target-period speed limit of R12) and replay then wins on reuse.
   Two candidate explanations for the K=100 result, not separated here: (a) stale replay targets (the buffer's actions and
   next-states come from older, worse policies) feed the collapse; (b) the recent-only learner never loses its failure
   transitions, since every failure it makes is in the next few batches. (b) is R11's mechanism and is the first thing to test.
