POST-HOC (not pre-registered). All analyses on median t_V per cell from results/summary.csv.

P1 linear law, T=inf, all lam<1 cells (30): t_V = 1.25 * D(1-lam) + 1.91, R2 = 0.999
   (slope ~1.25 rather than 1: with ~50% stall actions early on, V(s) rises geometrically toward V(s+1) each iteration, adding a constant fraction per state)

P2 harmonic model for T=16 cells (35): t_V = a*D*((1-lam) + 1/T_eff) + b with a=1.06, T_eff=6.1 states, b=0.61; R2 = 0.998
P3 min-horizon model (pre-registered form h_eff=min(T,1/(1-lam))) on the same cells: a=1.27, T_eff=6.1, b=2.08; R2 = 0.988
   -> the two slowdowns add as rates (1/h_eff = (1-lam) + 1/T_eff), they do not take a min. T_eff ~ states per segment, below 16 because stalls make 16 time steps cover fewer states and because fixed-time boundaries fall at different states across episodes.

P4 lam=0.99 vs lam=1 at T=16: [(8, 1.0), (8, 1.0), (16, 3.0), (16, 3.0), (32, 7.0), (32, 6.0), (64, 13.0), (64, 13.0), (128, 26.0), (128, 25.0)]
   rollout length alone sets the latency once lam is near 1.

P5 t_pi - t_V per cell (median iterations between V(s0) crossing 0.25 and success prob crossing 0.75):
   mean -0.34, min -2, max +1: the policy at s0 moves in the same iteration the critic does; there is no further policy lag.

P6 all 70 cells reach final success prob >= 0.98 by iteration 600 (min over cells: 0.983). Latency, not decay: no (lam, D) pair fails to learn.
