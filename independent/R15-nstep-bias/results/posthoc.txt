2. Reading. The max-bias term falls by 5-10x from n=1 to n=2 and is near zero by n=3 at sigma<=0.1; the pessimistic term grows
   roughly linearly in log n and is the same for plain and double Q (sigma=0 rows agree to +-0.005). So the fall of total
   overestimation with n is dominated by the max-bias shrinking (bootstrap weight gamma^n and fewer compounding steps), not by
   the pessimistic term growing. The two terms do have opposite signs, and the best n by AUC sits where their sum crosses zero
   (P4 held in both band and band2 at every noisy cell), which is the cancellation prediction in its weak form.

3. Double Q halves max-bias at n=1 (0.062 vs 0.132 at sigma=0.1 eps=0.1, band2; 0.020 vs 0.037 at sigma=0.05) but does not remove
   it, and shares the pessimistic term, so its slope on log n is 60-80% of plain Q's (K3 fails in all three grids). Double Q still
   benefits from n>1 under noise (best n = 3-5 at sigma=0.1).

4. Cost of pessimism at sigma=0: n=10 loses 11-13% (eps=0.1) and 22-23% (eps=0.3) of final return against n=1, for both methods.
   This is the price of uncorrected n-step in a deterministic-reward task and scales with eps as the T^mu story predicts.

5. Replication at sigma=0.1 across seed sets: best n = 3 for both eps in band (seeds 0-3) and band2 (seeds 4-7); plain-Q best
   final return 55% of V* (band) and 45% (band2). P3's 50% threshold was missed by the second seed set (45%), hence INCONCLUSIVE by rule.
