## R19. The credit-assignment speed limit of PPO

**Gap.** λ ≈ 0.95 and `num_steps` are set as variance knobs. In the iterated loop PPO actually runs, the critic is refit each iteration to λ-returns bootstrapped from itself, so reward information moves back ≈ 1/(1 − γλ) states per iteration, and a segment boundary at T steps adds a second cap. VC-PPO saw the symptom in long-CoT RLHF and called it decay. Nobody had written the iteration count, tested it, or noticed that rollout length reintroduces the same latency at λ = 1.

**Pilot.** Binary-choice corridor (reward at the end of one of two depth-D corridors, forward/stall actions, cap 3D, γ = 1). Tabular softmax policy with one SGD step per batch on normalised GAE advantages, exact tabular critic fit each iteration, segment length T ∈ {∞, 16}. D ∈ {8..128} × λ ∈ {0, 0.5, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 0.99, 1} × 8 seeds. Iterations until V(s₀) crosses half its value: **t_V = 1.25·D(1 − λ) + 1.9, R² = 0.999** over 30 cells (log-log slopes 0.95 in D, 0.88 in 1 − λ); λ = 1: one iteration at every depth. The start-state policy crosses its threshold in the same iteration (Spearman 0.99, lag −2..+1). Every one of 70 cells eventually solves the task; with the critic frozen at 0, λ = 0.95 never solves D = 128 and λ = 0.8 solves nothing — the critic is what turns decay into latency. Rollout length: T = 16 at λ = 1 scales as D^1.03 but is 1.5–2.3× slower than λ = 0.95 at T = ∞, so the pre-registered min(T, 1/(1 − λ)) form failed; post-hoc the slowdowns add as rates, 1/h_eff = (1 − λ) + 1/T_eff, T_eff ≈ 6 states per 16-step segment, R² = 0.998. At T = 16, λ = 0.99 and λ = 1 are indistinguishable.

**Reading.** 1/(1 − γλ) is an iterations-per-state speed, not a horizon. For long-horizon PPO the operative number is D·((1 − γλ) + 1/T_eff) critic fits before the first decision sees the reward at all. λ_critic = 1 (VC-PPO's fix) makes that one iteration only if the rollout covers the distance; MuJoCo-style `num_steps` = 128 on 1000-step episodes brings the latency back as D/T_eff. Critic-free group-baseline methods (GRPO-style) have zero latency by construction, which is one more reason they tolerate long horizons.

**What the paper needs.** Neural PPO (CleanRL) on long-corridor MiniGrid, λ × `num_steps` sweep, iteration at which the start-state advantage first correlates with the right action, tested against D((1 − γλ) + 1/T); learning-rate and epoch sweeps showing the latency is in iterations not samples; a small-LM frozen-terminal-reward proxy reproducing VC-PPO's observation as a latency curve; decoupled λ_actor/λ_critic confirming the law tracks λ_critic. One 3080 week.

**Why 40–45%.** The highest of any round: a closed-form law on the most-used algorithm in RL, confirmed at R² = 0.999 with a pre-registered test, with a direct bearing on the current RLHF debate (VC-PPO, GRPO) and a one-line practical rule. The critic-frozen contrast is the kind of figure reviewers remember. Risks: "tabular" and "obvious once said"; a neural critic may blur the constant enough that the law looks loose; VC-PPO's authors or others could publish the iteration-count framing first (scoop risk is the main one — move fast). The rollout-length half is genuinely new and not in VC-PPO.

## R20. Exploration or propagation?

**Gap.** Sparse-reward difficulty is diagnosed as exploration and treated with bonuses. Finding the reward once is not solving the task; the value has to reach the start through bootstrapping, and with a hard target refreshed every K updates that takes (⌈D/n⌉ − 1)K updates (R12). Nobody had measured the two halves separately.

**Pilot.** Corridors D ∈ {10, 20, 30}, ε = 0.1 with random greedy tie-breaking (untrained agent = random walk), tabular Q-learning with replay, target period K ∈ {1, 200, 2000} × n ∈ {1, 5} × count bonus β ∈ {0, 0.1} × 8 seeds, exact greedy evaluation. All 288 runs discover and solve. In the Nature-DQN-like cell (K = 2000, n = 1): discovery 346 / 1862 / 9324 steps, post-discovery **18 154 / 37 716 / 57 648** — ratios 52× / 20× / 6×, share 0.98 / 0.95 / 0.86. Median post-discovery time against R12's law: Spearman 0.92; two-term fit T_p = **0.96**·(⌈D/n⌉ − 1)K + 59·D²/(nB) + 584, R² = 0.996 — the post-discovery time is the target-sync count to within 4%. Fast target + n = 5 cuts total time-to-solve 31× / 18× / 7× with identical exploration. The count bonus (the exploration control) halved discovery at D = 10 and 20 but not 30 (K3 failed), shortened median discovery in only 5/18 cells, lengthened propagation in 16/18, and made total time-to-solve worse in 16/18 (D = 10, K = 1: 500 → 6500 steps).

**Reading.** Under standard value-based settings, on these tasks, most of "hard exploration" is post-discovery latency with a closed form, and the standard remedy attacks the smaller half while (as implemented, with stored stale bonuses) damaging the larger. This is consistent with what works in practice (Rainbow n = 3, R2D2 n = 5, Agent57's long traces) and gives a reason. The share is not a constant: it falls as discovery gets genuinely hard, and the claim is the decomposition plus the law for one half, not a percentage.

**What the paper needs.** DQN on MinAtar and MiniGrid hard-exploration tasks logging first extrinsic reward and first successful greedy episode, K × n × {none, count, RND}; a non-stale (recomputed at replay time) bonus to test the mechanism behind the propagation penalty; a "propagation burst" (temporarily large n after discovery) as a targeted intervention. The bonus control must be redone properly (β ∈ {0.3, 1}) before any claim about bonus methods. One 3080 week.

**Why 30–35%.** A reframing with a number attached, on a problem the whole exploration literature cares about, with a clean negative on the default remedy. Risks: tabular/corridor-specific and the propagation law is R12's (the new content is the accounting and the bonus trade-off, so the paper leans on the deep replication); the weak bonus control; reviewers from the exploration community may read it as hostile unless the MiniGrid version shows the share honestly shrinking on real hard-exploration tasks. If R12 and R20 end up as one submission (law + accounting), the combined odds are higher than either alone.

## Recommendation

R19 is now the top candidate of all eight rounds and should be first onto the 3080: the neural PPO replication is a one-week job and the result bears on a live debate. R20 is best submitted either merged with R12 (one paper: the target-sync latency law and what it explains, including the exploration accounting) or as the empirical half of a pair. The two together make a single story worth telling in a workshop talk even before the deep runs: in both families of deep RL, the time for a reward to reach a decision is a closed-form count of training iterations — critic fits in PPO, target syncs in DQN — and most of what looks like slow learning on long horizons is that count. Live main-track candidates across rounds: **R19**, **R12 (+R20)**, **R8**, **R11**, **R15**, **R16**, **R3**; short papers R13, R17; R7+R9 merged. The shared MinAtar harness from v7 (loss × sampler × reservoir × K) should add R20's logging (first reward, first greedy success) at zero cost.
